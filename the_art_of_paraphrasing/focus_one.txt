Flow, a term coined in psychology, describes moments at which the enjoyment of an activity is so intense that the person engaged in it feels as though he is drifting along with a current, with every movement seeming effortless.

Interviews with people from various professional domains have led to the conclusion that the experience of flow is the
same across cultures.

Statistical data indicate that U.S. teens are more likely to experience flow while playing sports or games and engaging in hobbies, at rates of 44% and 33%, respectively, whereas the figure for watching TV drops to 13 percent. Nonetheless, they spend four times as much time watching TV as they do playing sports or games and engaging in hobbies.


According to Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, adolescents from higher social classes seem to be less happy and enthusiastic than their lower-class peers.
