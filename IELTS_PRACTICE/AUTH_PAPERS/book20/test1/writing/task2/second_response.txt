Human dignity is the foundation of the right to life. In fact, that access to clean water is a basic human right is
indisputable. However, the proposal that the provision of water supply for every household should be free of charge
presents serious practical challenges. This essay will begin by describing the necessity of such access. Then,
it will refute the free water provision proposal.

It is clear that the right to water is intrinsically linked to the right to life. Indeed, with human organs
being highly dependent on water, death is inevitable within few days due to lack of it. In addition to this
reliance, water is the main means of sanitation, without which human beings are susceptible to infectious
diseases. 

However, such is the complexity of water supply system that its free provision faces serious challenges. First of
all, for each system to run effectively, there must be balance in what goes in and what goes out.  The discovery of
water resources, its transport to a reservoir where purification and maintenance take place, and its distribution
to every home are costly processes, for which financial resources should be taken care of, with service fees being
the most crucial income source for the system. Furthermore, even if enough financial resources are available
to governing body responsible for this systemic procedure, free access may lead to radical consumer behavior,
through which a large amount of supplied water could be wasted. Therefore, this proposal present challenges for not
only the social system but also for the ecosystem, in which water plays an important role.

To conclude, while the necessity of access to clean water is unquestionable, the argument that this access should
come with no cost is neither economically sustainable nor environmentally friendly.
