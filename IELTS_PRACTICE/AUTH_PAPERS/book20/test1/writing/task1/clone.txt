The first table provides information on total population changes in New York over the 19th and 20th centuries, while the second and third tables
exclusively show not only changes in the population of the five districts of the city but also their relative contributions to the total, over
the same time period.
                                                                                      
Overall, the total population of the city increased tenfold over the period. On the other hand, whereas the relative percentages of the four
districts to the total population experienced an upward trend, that of Manhattan showed a downward trend.

From 1800 to 1900, there was a dramatic increase in the population of New York City, with the figure rising from 79,216 to almost 3.5 million.
Similarly, but to a significantly lesser extent, the number of people living in New York increased over the following century, and by the end of the period, the figure stood at approximately 8 million.

In contrast, although the population of Manhattan also grew substantially in the 19th century, its relative
proportion decreased by 22 percentage points, from 76% to 54%. This decrease became even more significant in the 20th century, with only 19% of
the city’s population living in this district.

Meanwhile, the population of the four major districts—Brooklyn, Bronx, Queens, and Staten Island—rocketed from only 18,701 in 1800 to almost 1.5
million in 1900, and finally 6,471,089 in 2000. Likewise, their relative percentages rose from 24% in 1800 to 81% in 2000.
