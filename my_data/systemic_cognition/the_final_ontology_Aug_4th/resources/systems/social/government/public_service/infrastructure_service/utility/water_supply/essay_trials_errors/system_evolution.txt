Although there are different approaches to structuring written and spoken discourse on various issues, Tehranian Polymath has chosen systems thinking. However, systems can be quite complicated. Therefore, the initial focus shifts from the present to the past, when systems may have been in their infancy. Even more importantly, the creation of a man-made system is typically preceded by problems that lead to a need. Ultimately, a need gives rise to a purpose, which is the precursor to the design of a system. Accordingly, an evolutionary approach will be applied to almost all man-made systems, tracing their development to their current states and projecting their possible futures. In addition to this approach, natural systems associated with man-made systems will be dissected so that the knowledge gained can enhance the efficacy of our systemic framework.     

man-made water systems:
As humans are vulnerable beings, tribal living may have saved us in prehistoric times. But after the advent of agriculture, there was a growing tendency toward the creation of settlements, with some hunter-gatherers gradually beginning to give up their old, unpredictable lifestyle and embrace a new one in which they could reduce some of the uncertainty associated with hunting and gathering. However, the advantages of living in settlements came at a cost. According to Sedlak, with the development of settlements into villages and, eventually, into cities, access to critical resources, including potable water, became a major concern, as some parts of cities were located farther away from natural water resources. This problem was initially addressed by “digging wells” and “paying for the home delivery of water” (Water 4.0, p. 1).
