📝 IELTS Writing Task 2: Potable Water Questions
Each question requires a formal essay (250+ words), with clear position, developed ideas, and examples.
1. Access & Human Rights
"Access to clean drinking water should be a basic human right. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
Key angles:
Health and dignity
Global inequality (e.g., sub-Saharan Africa vs. Europe)
Government duty vs. personal responsibility
Link to SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation)
2. Urban Infrastructure Challenges
"In many cities, the supply of clean drinking water cannot keep up with population growth. What problems does this cause, and how can they be solved?"
Systemic causes:
Aging pipes → contamination/leakage
Groundwater depletion
Poor wastewater treatment
Urban sprawl without planning
Solutions:
Smart metering
Rainwater harvesting
Public awareness campaigns
Investment in purification tech
3. Bottled Water vs. Tap Water
"Many people prefer bottled water over tap water. Is this a positive or negative development?"
Arguments for "negative":
Environmental cost (plastic waste, carbon footprint)
High cost → inequity
Misconception about purity (some bottled water = filtered tap)
Arguments for "positive":
Safe where tap water is polluted
Convenient for travelers
Encourages hydration
4. Government vs. Private Control
"Should the supply of clean drinking water be managed by governments or private companies? Discuss both views and give your opinion."
Government pros:
Ensures universal access
Prioritizes public health over profit
Can subsidize low-income users
Private pros:
More efficient operations
Innovation through competition
Reduced tax burden
Your stance: Often best hybrid model
5. Individual Responsibility
"The best way to ensure clean drinking water for everyone is through individual actions like conservation. Do you agree?"
Disagree (systemic view):
Individuals can save water, but won’t fix broken infrastructure
Corporations/agriculture use 70% of freshwater
Only policy change prevents large-scale pollution
Agree (if narrow focus):
Cultural shift starts with individuals
Public pressure → policy change
Conservation reduces strain
6. Climate Change Impact
"Climate change is making it harder to provide clean drinking water. What measures should governments take to address this challenge?"
Measures:
Invest in desalination plants
Protect watersheds from deforestation
Upgrade storage and distribution networks
Promote drought-resistant crops
Support transboundary water cooperation
7. Pollution & Contamination
"Water pollution is one of the most serious environmental problems today. Why does it happen, and what can be done to reduce it?"
Causes:
Industrial discharge
Agricultural runoff (pesticides, fertilizers)
Untreated sewage
Plastic microfibers
Solutions:
Stricter regulations
Wastewater recycling
Public education
Green technology adoption
8. Global Inequality
"Millions of people around the world lack access to clean drinking water. Why is this still happening, and what can be done to solve it?"
Root causes:
Poverty
Weak infrastructure
Conflict zones
Climate vulnerability
Solutions:
International aid
Local well-drilling projects
Solar-powered purifiers
Education on hygiene
9. Technology & Innovation
"Technological solutions such as water purification systems and desalination plants can solve the problem of unsafe drinking water. How far do you agree?"
Balanced answer:
Tech helps, but not enough alone
Desalination = energy-intensive
Purification works only if maintained
Need policy + education + equity
10. Historical Comparison
"In the past, people used natural sources like rivers and wells for drinking water. Today, we rely on treated water systems. Has this change been beneficial overall?"
Yes:
Dramatically reduced cholera/dysentery
Safer, more reliable supply
Supports urban life
But consider:
Loss of connection to nature
Over-reliance on complex systems
Risk of systemic failure (e.g., Flint, Michigan)
