VERB: HAVE
│
├── 1. PRIMARY MEANING CATEGORIES (General)
│   ├── Possession → "I have a book"
│   ├── Composition → "The cell has a nucleus"
│   ├── Experience (Event-Based) → "I had dinner"
│   └── State Encounter → "I had trouble..." ← You are here
│
└── 2. SUBCATEGORY: HAVE + Abstract Obstacle
    │   // Signifies resistance to volitional action
    │
    ├── TROUBLE DOMAIN
    │   ├── have trouble (doing X)
    │   ├── have difficulty (with Y)
    │   ├── have problems (when Z)
    │   └── have challenges (in A)
    │
    ├── NEUROCOGNITIVE INTERPRETATION
    │   ├── Volition present → "I wanted to get up"
    │   ├── Motor intent formed → Prefrontal cortex active
    │   ├── Execution delayed/blocked → due to fatigue, depression, inertia
    │   └── Gap recognized → hence "trouble"
    │
    ├── ONTOLOGICAL STATUS OF "TROUBLE"
    │   ├── Not physical object → immaterial obstacle
    │   ├── Emergent from system-state mismatch:
    │   │   Desired state: awake, upright
    │   │   Actual state: tired, low energy
    │   └── Therefore: "Trouble" = systemic friction
    │
    ├── MODALITY OF CONTROL
    │   ├── Partial agency → "I could eventually get up"
    │   ├── Impaired initiation → akin to "akinesia" (lack of movement) in Parkinson’s
    │   └── Not full volitional failure → still possible after effort
    │
    └── FUNCTION IN SENTENCE
        ├── Questions availability of smooth transition
        ├── Implies expectation: "getting up should be easy"
        ├── Reveals normative assumption about daily functioning
        └── Invites reflection on biological readiness vs. social demand
